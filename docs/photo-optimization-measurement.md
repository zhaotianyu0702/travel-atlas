# Photo optimization measurement

A synthetic 2400 × 1600 RGB gradient with deterministic high-frequency noise was encoded locally with libvips/sharp, resized to 1920 × 1280 and encoded as JPEG quality 72. The source PNG was 10,023,705 bytes; the JPEG was 273,018 bytes (97.3% smaller). PSNR against the resized synthetic source was 33.04 dB. This is a synthetic input and a different JPEG encoder from browser canvas: it is not a measurement of personal photos, perceptual quality, or a guarantee of a particular size. Complex images can exceed the 650 KiB target even after the fallback steps.

Optimized uploads preserve only locally extracted capture time, explicit offset when available, and GPS as structured fields. Other EXIF, maker notes, depth data, animation, transparency and the source image's original bitstream are not preserved in the optimized JPEG. The source SHA-256 is used for duplicate detection; it is not a hash of the stored JPEG. Choose Keep originals to retain the uploaded source file. Original files remain on the user's device regardless of this option.

Three existing CC0 travel JPEG fixtures (Rome, Lisbon and Paris, each already resized to 1280 pixels in the upstream demo) were also run through the same approximate settings in libvips. The JPEG archive fell from 244–334 KiB to 150–197 KiB. Including the separate 800 px/0.69 browse thumbnail, the pair took 212–277 KiB, **13–17% smaller** than these already-compressed inputs. The isolated archive re-encode kept its original dimensions on these fixtures; PSNR was 30.88–32.54 dB, a pixel metric rather than a guarantee of visual equivalence. New optimized photos display the higher resolution archive when opened individually.
